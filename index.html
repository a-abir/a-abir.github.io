01 — about

Mechanical engineer with a strong software background, developed through building custom measurement and automation systems for research and production environments.

My work spans manufacturing, software, controls, and experimental research — designing sensor systems, automating multi-physics simulation workflows, and developing data analysis methods for process characterization.

Currently an Applications Engineer for Advanced Manufacturing at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, where I design, install, and calibrate image-based and multi-sensor monitoring systems, and develop the data acquisition software that ensures dataset quality for downstream analysis and model training.

Previously a Process Engineer I at DENSO Manufacturing Tennessee, where I led automation and controls projects on an automotive electronics production line — including new product introduction, equipment specification, vision-guided robotics, capacity studies, and IATF 16949 documentation.

My graduate research at the University of Tennessee focused on in-situ process monitoring for additive friction stir deposition — detecting defects and metallurgical changes from machine telemetry in a process where the tool head occludes direct optical observation. Prior experience includes tokamak probe cooling optimization at ORNL Fusion, nuclear fuel failure prediction at GE Vernova Hitachi, and four years of materials characterization research in Dr. Brett Compton's lab.

05 — engineering philosophy

Engineering principles

01

Start with data.

Hypotheses about process behavior should be evaluated against recorded telemetry rather than debated from experience alone. Measured data provides an objective basis for engineering decisions.

02

Automate repeatable work.

Tasks that execute more than twice should be automated. The lasting value of the tokamak optimization was the reusable workflow, not any single optimized geometry. Automated tools outlast the projects that created them.

03

Validate against hardware.

A simulation is a hypothesis encoded in software. Converged solutions can be confidently wrong when boundary conditions do not reflect the physical setup. When model predictions and experimental measurements disagree, the measurement takes precedence.

04

Root cause analysis requires depth.

Component replacement that resolves a symptom is not root cause identification. The relevant question is why that component failed in that position at that point in the cycle — and whether the failure mode will recur.

05 — unofficial

Document the solution.

Undocumented solutions are repeatedly re-derived at significant cost. Thorough documentation converts individual problem-solving into organizational knowledge.

06 — things i find interesting

Areas of technical interest

manufacturing systems

System-level constraints in production lines are often misidentified. Optimizing individual machines is less impactful than identifying and addressing the actual bottleneck.

fusion energy

Plasma-facing component engineering — the materials and thermal management challenges of building structures that survive sustained plasma exposure.

robotics

The engineering challenge of bridging demonstrated capability and reliable production-floor operation across all shifts and conditions.

computer vision

Industrial vision system deployment, where illumination and fixturing design typically determine performance more than algorithm selection.

simulation

Boundary condition specification and validation. Governing equations are well-established; solution accuracy depends primarily on how faithfully boundary conditions represent the physical system.

failure analysis

Fracture surface analysis and failure mode characterization. A fracture surface encodes the loading history and failure sequence in its morphology.

reverse engineering

Characterizing undocumented protocols, black-box controllers, and legacy equipment to enable integration and continued operation.

statistical distance metrics

Distributional distance measures, particularly the Wasserstein (earth mover's) distance, for process monitoring and change detection applications.

intermittent faults

Observation-dependent failures — typically caused by timing sensitivity, grounding issues, or probe loading effects. Diagnosis requires non-invasive measurement techniques.

unit consistency

Unit conversion errors in engineering systems — from the Mars Climate Orbiter to plant-level incidents. A persistent source of preventable failures.

FIRST Robotics

Four years as team lead, 30+ students mentored. An effective introduction to systems engineering under fixed deadlines with real hardware constraints.

teaching

90+ students through ME 449 and EF 151/152/230. Teaching is an effective method for identifying gaps in one's own understanding of fundamentals.
